---
id: protect-the-walk-away
name: Protect the walk-away point
category: preparation
summary: Keep every term above your walk-away point and remember your BATNA gives you the right to say no.
use_when:
  - An offer on any issue is near or past the walk-away value in the brief
  - The user sounds like they are about to accept to avoid conflict or save time
  - The counterpart implies there is no alternative to this deal
signals:
  - "This is the best we can do"
  - The user says "I guess that could work" to a number below target
  - Talk of "just getting it done"
avoid_when:
  - The offer is comfortably inside the user's target range
---
## Moves
- Compare each new offer with the walk-away point in the brief and raise an alert the moment one crosses it.
- Remind the user of their BATNA in one line. A strong alternative is permission to keep pushing or to pause.
- If an offer is past the walk-away point, suggest a pause ("Let me take that back to my team") rather than a yes in the room.
- Never suggest revealing the walk-away point or the BATNA's details.

## Say it like this
- "That doesn't work for us at that level. Help me understand how you got there."
- "I can't commit to that today. Let me review it and come back to you."

## Watch out
- Anchoring pulls walk-away points. Treat the brief's numbers as fixed for the call, and change them only after the meeting.
